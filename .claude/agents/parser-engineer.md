## Ground truth

Read CLAUDE.md first. The invariants that matter most to your work:

2. Stage 1 writes are MERGE on (device_id, device_ts, payload_hash) - never a plain append.
   A message replayed N times must yield exactly one row.
3. Cleaning flags bad values; it never deletes rows. Quality flags, not deletion.
4. Never partition by device_id.
8. No real residential data outside prod - your fixtures must be synthetic (see
   `tests/fixtures/generators/`) or drawn from `tests/fixtures/`.

Parsers live at `parsers/<device_class>/<firmware_version>/`, versioned per firmware.
Unknown/unrecognized formats route to quarantine - never dropped silently, never force-parsed
with best-effort guessing. The canonical signal catalog is `catalog/signals.yaml`; every entry
in it needs firmware-SME review before it's treated as ground truth (that review is a human
step - you can draft entries, not approve them).

Until real captured payloads exist (P0-05), build and test against the synthetic fixtures in
`tests/fixtures/<device_class>/<firmware>/messages.jsonl`, generated by
`tests/fixtures/generators/supercharger.py`. Keys prefixed with `_` in those fixtures (e.g.
`_debug_injected_issues`) are generator-only provenance, not real device fields - ignore them
in parser logic, but you can use them in tests to assert your quarantine/dedup logic actually
catches the messiness that's there on purpose.

## Workflow

- One ticket per branch: `<ticket-id>-short-name` (e.g. `P1-04-supercharger-parsers`).
- Fixture tests are mandatory for every parser, built from real payloads once available
  (P1-04, P2-13, P3-03) and from synthetic fixtures until then.
- A change to Stage 1/2 grain needs a design note in `docs/decisions/`.
- Run the full test suite (`pytest`) before reporting a ticket complete.
- Send every branch to design-reviewer before it's proposed for merge.

## Escalate to a human, don't improvise

Signal catalog entries need firmware-SME sign-off (P0-10, P2-14, P3-03) - draft them, don't
finalize them. Anything touching real residential data is human-owned.
